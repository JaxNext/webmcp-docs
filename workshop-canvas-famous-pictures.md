# Famous & Interesting Pictures the Whiteboard Demo Can Draw

The demo editor can draw only **rectangles, ellipses, lines, freehand paths, and
text**, with a stroke or fill of any color and a variable stroke width. That
primitive set is surprisingly close to a century of geometric abstract art — and
to some of the world's most recognizable logos. Each entry below names a picture,
the keyword to look it up, and a link, plus which tools reproduce it.

## The headline: Sol LeWitt's "instructions" are agent tool calls

Sol LeWitt (1928–2007) pioneered wall drawings that exist *only as written
directions*, executed by other people rather than the artist himself. His
instructions read like a program — "a one-inch grid… within each square, a line
in one of four directions"\[cite:3] — and none of the drawings were made by his
own hand\[cite:2]. This is the single best art-historical anchor for the
workshop: **a LeWitt wall drawing is to a wall what a WebMCP tool call is to a
canvas** — a set of precise, executable instructions carried out by someone (or
something) else.

## More styles that fit the primitives

### Grids of squares (`rect` + `fill`)

- ✕ **Josef Albers — Homage to the Square** (1950–1976) · keyword: *Albers squares* · three or four squares nested inside one another and offset toward the bottom[cite:4] · <https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/489056>
- **Bridget Riley — Movement in Squares** (1961) · keyword: *Movement in Squares* · twelve rows of alternating black and white squares that compress toward a vertical line, folding the picture plane[cite:5] · <https://pictorx.com/about-op-art>
- **Vera Molnár — algorithmic grids** (1968–) · keyword: *Vera Molnar* · squares, circles and lines generated by a "predetermined set of mathematical rules"[cite:6] · <https://ropac.net/artists/231->
- **Piet Mondrian — Broadway Boogie Woogie** (1942–43) · keyword: *Broadway Boogie Woogie* · a dense grid of small coloured rectangles, no black lines at all · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Boogie_Woogie>

### Dots and circles (`ellipse` + `fill`)

- ✕ **Damien Hirst — spot paintings** (1986–) · keyword: *Damien Hirst spots* · evenly spaced dots of uniform size in a rigid grid, no two colours alike · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Hirst>
- ✕ **Yayoi Kusama — polka dots / Infinity Nets** · keyword: *Kusama polka dots* · fields of repeated dots that "read as nets or as dots depending on whether the eye focuses on the paint or on the gaps"[cite:7] · <https://www.tate.org.uk/tate-etc/issue-24-spring-2012/infinity-on-single-canvas>
- ✕ **Roy Lichtenstein — Ben-Day dots** (1961–) · keyword: *Ben-Day dots* · a regular dot screen used to fake tone, "reinforc[ing] the printed nature" of the image[cite:8] · <https://www.moma.org/artists/3542-roy-lichtenstein>

### Lines only (`line`)

- ✕ **Barnett Newman — "zip" paintings** (1948–) · keyword: *Barnett Newman zip* · one or more thin vertical strips of colour cutting a monochrome field[cite:9] · <http://www.moma.org/collection/works/79601>
- **François Morellet — systematic line distributions** · keyword: *François Morellet lines* · grids of lines displaced by a rule, from ordered to random · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Morellet>
- **Bridget Riley — line works** (*Fall*, *Current*) · keyword: *Bridget Riley lines* · thin lines whose spacing warps the surface — needs many short segments here, since the editor has no curves[cite:5] · <https://pictorx.com/about-op-art>

### Text (`text`)

- ✕ **Ed Ruscha — word paintings** · keyword: *Ed Ruscha word painting* · a single word or phrase floating on a flat colour field · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Ruscha>
- ✕ **Jenny Holzer — Truisms** (1977–) · keyword: *Jenny Holzer Truisms* · short aphorisms set as plain type, often one per line · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Holzer>
- ✕ **Robert Indiana — LOVE** (1964–) · keyword: *LOVE sculpture Indiana* · four letters stacked in a square, O tilted · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOVE_(sculpture)>
- ✕ **Barbara Kruger — text blocks** · keyword: *Barbara Kruger* · white type on a solid red rectangle · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Kruger>

### Geometric composition (all three primitives)

- **El Lissitzky — Proun compositions** (1919–) · keyword: *Proun Lissitzky* · two- and three-dimensional geometric elements "projected on a plane surface, creating dynamic tensions"[cite:10] · <https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/cEbzRoL>
- ✕ **Kazimir Malevich — Suprematism** (1915–) · keyword: *Malevich suprematism* · black square, coloured rectangles and circles on white, no representation at all · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suprematism>

### Formats, not movements

These aren't art styles but structures that map almost perfectly onto the editor:

- ✕ **National flags** · keyword: *simple flag designs* · Japan (a red circle on white), Bangladesh, Palau, Switzerland (a cross), the Nordic crosses, France and Greece (bands) — pure `rect` + `ellipse`, and a nice test of exact coordinates.
- ✕ **Transit maps** · keyword: *Harry Beck tube map* · coloured lines at 45°/90° with station dots — exercises `line` + small `ellipse` together.
- **Pixel art / 8-bit sprites** · keyword: *pixel art sprite* · a grid of filled squares, the most literal use of `rect` + `fill`.
- ✕ **Quilt patterns** · keyword: *Gee's Bend quilt* · bold geometric blocks of solid colour, often asymmetric.
- ✕ **Charts** · keyword: *bar chart* · bars are rectangles, a scatter plot is dots, a pie chart is… not possible without arcs — a useful "what the tool can't do" example.

## Why this matters for the workshop

Every picture above is composed of the demo's exact primitives, so an attendee
can watch the agent reconstruct a *recognizable famous image* rather than a
stick figure. The narrative arc writes itself: **Sol LeWitt → Albers → Hirst →
Riley → Lissitzky**, each a harder exercise in turning a verbal description into
precise tool calls — which is precisely what WebMCP makes possible and MCP
cannot.
